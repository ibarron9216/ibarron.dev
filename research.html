Overview

My research focuses on signal processing methods for security, privacy, and authentication. Much of this work asks how familiar visual systems, especially QR codes and barcodes, can carry information in ways that are useful to people while still limiting what an unintended viewer can recover.

My publications on dual-modulated QR codes and specialized barcodes study proximity-based communication: visual codes that can be read normally from a distance, but reveal protected information only at close range. Recent work extends that line into self-authenticating QR codes designed to help defend against QR phishing attacks.

I have also worked on digital provenance, including a blockchain and image-forensics approach for authenticating and tracking fine art paintings. Across these projects, the goal is practical trust: making authentication and privacy tools that can operate in real settings, not only in controlled demonstrations.
